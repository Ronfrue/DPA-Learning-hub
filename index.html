Where the DPA departs from the GDPR

The DPA borrows its structure from the UK Data Protection Act 1998 and much of its substance from the GDPR, but several obligations are heavier in Jamaica. A GDPR-compliant programme is a good start, not a finished one.

Sources and how to use them

Section and regulation summaries paraphrase the Data Protection Act, 2020 and the three 2024 Regulations as published by the Office of the Information Commissioner. Always confirm against the gazetted text before relying on a point.

Primary law (Office of the Information Commissioner)

  • The Data Protection Act, 2020
  • The Data Protection Regulations, 2024 (Minister's Regulations)
  • Data Protection (Data Controller Registration) Regulations, 2024
  • Disposal of Personal Data Regulations, 2024
  • OIC: The Data Protection Standards
  • Parliament of Jamaica copy of the Act (PDF)

Comparative law and good practice

  • Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the GDPR
  • European Data Protection Board guidelines (consent, breach notification, DPIAs, transfers)
  • UK Information Commissioner's Office guidance, the closest analogue given the DPA's UK lineage
  • ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 27701 (information security and privacy information management)
  • NIST Privacy Framework

Secondary commentary

  • Chambers, Data Protection & Privacy 2026: Jamaica
  • RecordingLaw, Jamaica Data Privacy Laws guide
  • JIS, Registration fees established for data controllers

Limits

This hub is a learning and analysis aid. It does not replace the gazetted text, OIC guidance or legal advice. Section and regulation summaries were checked against the OIC copies of the Act, the Data Protection Regulations, 2024, the Data Controller Registration Regulations, 2024 and the Disposal of Personal Data Regulations, 2024 (Gazette, 17 September 2025). The only point resting on commentary is the Morrison v Elephant Group case report.
